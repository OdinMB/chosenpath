### To manipulate and get information from the StoryState, use model classes

- Relevant model classes include Story.ts and ClientStateManager.ts, PlayerManager.ts, and ThreadManager.ts (all in shared/models directory).
- If these classes don't yet have a function to manipulate or get the required information from StoryState, add new functions to the classes.
- Don't add any complicated StoryState browsing and manipulation logic in other parts of the app.
- Keep story manipulation logic centralized in these domain models

### Checks and repairs on model replies

The game keeps what a model reply means, not only what matches an id exactly. Each repair is a `Repair` record (`server/src/game/services/textRepairs.ts`); `logRepairs` prints one `[LLM] repair` line per reply with the role, story id, turn and the count per kind (notes apart). The line carries no story text and no model-written ids; the records do, for tests and the eval, which checks replies after these repairs and counts them per kind (`.context/text-model-eval.md`, "Checks on what the game keeps").

**Beat replies** (`server/src/game/services/beatRepairs.ts`, `repairBeatReply`). `AIStoryGenerator.generateBeats` runs it on every reply, pregeneration siblings included, before `beatStep.apply`, so the beats are stored already repaired. `ChangeService` still applies exact ids only.

- Stat changes: a player stat in seat form (`player1_energy`, `player_player_energy`) is read as the plain id under that seat; a shared stat under a player moves to `shared`; in single player every player-stat change goes to player1. A player stat under `shared` in multiplayer, or a seat form under another player's group, is ambiguous and dropped; unknown stats are dropped. Both are counted.
- A `setString` on a number, percentage or opposites stat whose text reads as one value (read as a background value is: `"a|b"` summing to 100 is a, `"40%"` is 40, `"12"` is 12) becomes the `setNumber` the game applies (`statNumberAsText`). ChangeService drops a text on those types, so before this a switch turn that moved a contest's scoreboard as `setString "35|65"` left the score at 50 (setup round 3's chain). A signed value (`"-10%"`, `"+5"`, or a number below 0) is a change by so much, not a value to set, and is left as it was (ChangeService drops it); read as a value it would set health to 0 or gold to 5.
- A `setString` value its stat's non-blank `possibleValues` don't mention is a note (`offLadderValue`); the change still applies.
- A contest's scoreboard moves only toward the side that won the contest step or chapter the turn follows (`scoreboardDirection`, since 2026-09-30; `scoreboards.ts`). The scoreboard is the shared opposites stat the contested outcome's resonance names ("Scored by …"): setup round 3's form (production since 2026-09-28), which also puts player1's side (with three players, player1's camp) first, so its value is that side's share and up is toward it. Only a contest with player1 on side A is read (the plan check's PL-11 puts them there when they are in it). Left as written: a story set up before round 3, which names no scoreboard and whose opposites stats were meters for what changes often, with either side first (the old fallback to the story's one shared opposites stat turned correct moves around on those); and a contest player1 sits out, whose sides are the planner's and whose camp no check reads. The result followed: at a switch or the ending, the chapter that just ended (`getResolvedThreadAnalysis`); in a chapter, the step just played (none on its first step). A move the other way is turned around by the same size (an addition becomes a subtraction and back; a value set is reflected around the score before, within 0 to 100): the model sized the move, the recorded result says which way. Moves are read in order, each from the score the ones before it leave, after the `statNumberAsText` repair. After a mixed result, where two contests on one scoreboard disagree (or one of them is not read), and on turns that follow no contest result, moves stay as written (the setup's rule moves a scoreboard only after a thread about its contest, none after a mixed result, which this doesn't enforce). A contest turned into one side's challenge (PL-12) is not read: which side its players are on isn't stored. Found by the playthroughs (food trucks turn 22: after side B's win the reply's own reasoning said "15 points toward" side B and it wrote `addNumber 15`, 35|65 → 50|50).
- Stat bonuses (`modifiersToSuccessRate`, on every challenge option, the ending's too): an id `Story.getStatById` already resolves is kept (the exact id, a shared stat, or the seat form `player1_energy` the bonus contract asks for). One it doesn't (the doubled `player1_player_skills_traits` or `player_player_skills_traits`, which GPT-6 wrote for 14-20% of counted bonuses; a seat on a stat whose id has no `player_`) is read as the stat changes' seat form is and written as the contract asks, `player1_skills_traits` (`bonusStatIdSeatForm`), or as the plain id where the stat's id has no `player_` to replace, so the roll breakdown players see names the stat instead of showing the raw id. The seat is the option's own; in single player every seat is the one player's. In multiplayer another player's seat is ambiguous (`bonusStatAmbiguous`) and a seat the story lacks unknown (`bonusStatUnknown`), and so is anything else unresolved: those stay as written and are notes, since the game counts a bonus's points whatever its id.
- Facts: kept on `world`, a story element or an element created in the same reply. A fact filed under a stat, an outcome or a player slot becomes a world fact prefixed with the stat's name, the outcome's question or the character's name; any other id becomes an unprefixed world fact.
- Introductions of an element that neither exists nor is created in the reply are dropped.
- Milestones: the group comes from the outcome (shared first; an id held in two lists is noted). An unknown id is mapped only when exactly one ended thread's outcome has no milestone in the reply and exactly one milestone has an unknown id; otherwise it is dropped. On turns that may add milestones (`canAddMilestones` in `storyTextSteps.ts`: the ending, or a switch after the first beat), an ended thread (`Story.getResolvedThreadAnalysis()`) whose outcome got fewer milestones than threads that pushed it gets its planned `thread.milestone`; exploration threads record theirs too (`ThreadResolutionService.getMilestone`).
- In a group turn, a sacrifice or reward that draws on a shared stat goes to one player per turn (`sharedLeverRepeated`, since 2026-09-30): the first seat offered it keeps it, and each later seat's copy of the same lever (sacrifice or reward) on the same stat leaves that player's options, so they choose from two. An option names its lever's stat only in its text, so the stat is read as the eval's lever readings read it: the stats the text names (a plural name also without its "s"), else those whose held value it names ("Ivo Senn" in the crew's Dockside Favors), else the one stat that allows that lever; of those, the ones that allow it; none unless exactly one is left, and then nothing changes. Only in a challenge set: in an exploration set an option's position is its result, so the copy stays and is noted (`sharedLeverRepeatedKept`). A lever on a player's own stat is every player's own, and a sacrifice and a reward of one stat are different levers. Found by the playthroughs (space pirates turn 16: "Call in Ivo Senn's dockside favor" offered to two players in one shared challenge, the crew's one favour; both took its +30).
- Option types follow the player's phase (`expectedOptionType`): exploration at switches and in exploration threads, challenge in challenge and contest threads; the ending is untouched. A converted challenge option gets risk "normal", no bonuses, and base 0, or the fixed sacrifice / reward points.

**The planners (planner v2, since 2026-09-28).** The switch and chapter planners write lean replies (`plannerReplies.ts`), which the request's `assemble` turns into the stored `SwitchAnalysis` / `ThreadAnalysis` before the plan check and the story read them. A topic direction is a sentence plus the one outcome it pushes (from the story's outcome ids as a list), stored as today's `"text (outcome id)"` string with the pair beside it (`topicDirections`); the player's pick sets the next chapter's outcome in code (`pacing.ts`, `pickedOutcome`: a flavor switch's outcome, or the chosen direction's by position), so a single player's chapter plan writes no outcome; a group's plan names it, from the ids as a list, and the plan check holds it. When a single player's pick names no outcome the story knows (the switch turn always offers three options, so a switch planned with two directions gets an invented third; or a switch planned before planner v2 whose chosen direction names no id), the chapter takes a fallback (`fallbackOutcomeId`: the outcome that still needs the most milestones, the main outcome on a tie, then the first listed), which PLAYER DECISIONS and PACING name and the assembly stores; the plan check notes it (`chapterOutcomeFallback`). Without it the reply, which has no outcome field, would fail the check on both attempts and stop the story at that switch. Both planners read a PACING block the game computes (`pacing.ts`: turns left, threads that fit (turns left ÷ 4, and since 2026-09-30 never fewer than the length rule makes come, `fewestThreads`: at 3 turns left "about 0 more threads fit" had stood beside "exactly 2 beats"), milestones still needed with the ended chapter pending, complete outcomes, the main outcome, recent lengths, the phase, and for the chapter planner the lengths allowed and the last chapter); priority is binding late in the story (the owner's call). A chapter carries its kind, question and plan beside today's fields. Since the owner's feedback of 2026-09-28 (planner v2c) the chapter's question is nearer than its outcome's (its three milestones answer it, about the chapter's own situation: a place, a person, a deadline, an object; never the outcome's question reworded, and a topic switch's chosen direction is narrowed to it too, since a direction can restate its outcome), and the planner writes the kind of milestone itself (`typeOfMilestone`, the concrete thing the answer settles; the question stands in where a reply leaves it empty). Since 2026-09-30 (planner v2e, the owner's feedback of 2026-09-29 and decision of 2026-09-30) the chapter names its outcome's stages (`outcomeStages`, one per intended milestone, stored on the thread and read by nothing yet) and stays within the one PACING says it settles: an outcome with n intended milestones has n stages, and the chapter settles stage k = milestones so far + 1 (`stageOf` in `pacing.ts`; none once the outcome is complete, when the chapter is an aftermath), so a story saved mid-story reads its stage from the milestones it holds. The story's last chapter settles only its outcome's next stage too, never the whole outcome at once, and the ending tells each outcome as its milestones leave it (the owner's decision, production since 2026-09-30: `ENDING_OUTCOME_RULE` and `ENDING_OUTCOME_KINDS` in `BeatPromptService.ts`, with each outcome's standing after the ending's milestones from `endingStates.ts`, complete or unfinished, k of n, counting one milestone per resolved thread on the outcome, which the state's "Milestones (k / n)" leaves out; a complete outcome resolved as its milestones point, an unfinished one in its current state, even if inconclusive, never beyond its milestones, in the story's own words). The reply lists each step once (the steps before the last, then the last step alone): planners had written the last step twice, making a chapter a beat longer. Since the choice-result stage of 2026-09-30 (planner v2f) each step result follows a rule for its kind. A challenge or contest result, the milestones included, says how the attempt turns out (what the player or each side achieves or fails to achieve, and how others respond), never which approach the player takes or what they say or decide: the result is rolled against the option the player chose, and a result that names an action had the next turn tell the rolled action over the chosen one (the playthroughs' New Avalon hearing chapters: "Eli plainly retracts" against "Eli defends his framing"). An exploration result is something the player chooses to do, since it is the option at its position, never how others respond. Measured: results that fit their kind 8 of 33 → 18 of 33. Today's form before the adoption is kept for the eval only (`storyTextRound0/`).

**Switch and thread plans** (`server/src/game/services/planChecks.ts`). `AIStoryGenerator.generateSwitches` / `generateThreads` call the planner through `checkedSwitchPlan` / `checkedThreadPlan`: `checkSwitchPlan` / `checkThreadPlan` (pure) repair the reply where the fix is unambiguous and name what can't be used; a plan with a problem gets one more call with `Your previous plan could not be used: <problem>. Write the plan again.` appended to the prompt, and a second problem throws `UnusableResultError` into the turn's failure path. Pregenerated plans take the same path. `checkedThreadPlan` also reads the chapter length PACING allows (`checkThreadPlan(..., { lengths: true })`, a `lengthProblem` apart from `problem`): a length outside the allowed ones gets the retry too, but never fails the turn: a second reply whose only problem is its length is used (note `lengthNotAllowed`), and so is a first reply whose only problem was its length when the second can't be used at all or its call fails (after the chat model's own re-sends; a warning names the error's class, never its message). The eval's plan readings call the check without the option, as it was measured.

- Switches: a player stays in the first switch that lists them (a slot the story doesn't have is dropped, a switch left empty goes); a player in no switch is a problem. A flavor switch's directions are cleared; an unknown outcome or a blank question is a problem. Topic directions that are blank, under 12 letters or a piece of the reply's JSON (a field name followed by a quote, a colon or nothing; a quote followed by a colon) are dropped, and so are directions whose bracketed id-like words are all unknown; the first three stay, fewer than two is a problem. Whatever is dropped or trimmed leaves `topicDirections` too, so the options the switch turn offers and the outcomes the pick reads stay in step by position (a list out of step with the strings is dropped, and the pick reads the strings). Directions whose text names two or more known outcomes, or none, are kept and noted: planner v2 writes each direction's one outcome in its own field, so the notes fire on switches planned before it and on a direction whose text names another outcome's id.
- Threads: a player listed in several threads stays in one. Since 2026-09-30 (`threadsKept`), that is the first on the outcome their switch pick named (`pickedOutcome`), else the first that lists them. A kept thread other than the first is noted `threadKeptOnPick`. A single player, and every group in the story's first chapter (every player together by rule), keeps the first. Before, it was always the first, so a planner that put two players into the contest a third player picked, as well as into the threads they picked, dropped both of their picks (the playthroughs' space pirates, turn 6). A player in no thread is a problem. Duplicate ids get a numeric suffix. A step before a thread's last that is an exact copy of the last one (the same title and question, whitespace aside) is dropped and the last, whose results are the milestones, stays (PL-14, `lastStepRepeated`, since 2026-09-30: planners wrote the last step in `steps` and again as `finalStep`, a chapter a beat longer that plays its moment twice; planner v2e 2 of 46 plans). Only where that gives the plan no problem the written one didn't have: threads of different lengths, a thread outside 2 to 4 steps, or a length PACING doesn't allow where the written one it did; then every copy stays, noted `lastStepRepeatedKept`. A relabelled or reworded copy is not exact and stays. When every thread has the same number of steps, 2 to 4, the duration is taken from it; otherwise a problem.
- A contest only one side's players are in becomes that side's challenge (PL-12, `oneSidedAsChallenge`, since 2026-09-30; repair `contestOneSided`). It applies to a group whose other players are in threads of their own. Its results become favorable (that side's win), mixed and unfavorable (the other side's win), its players go on side A, and a stored `kind` says challenge. Found by the playthroughs: where a group's players picked different directions and only one side picked the contest, the planner wrote it with that side alone, the check refused it, its one retry repeated it and the turn failed for good. The text stays the contest's, so a step or result can still name the absent side's player (unmeasured). In a contest game, a one-sided contest holding every player stays a problem (the opponents are missing, not elsewhere; a first chapter's contest is one). In a cooperative group, which plays no contest, contest results on one side are that challenge whoever is in it (this closes the code review's open case of a cooperative story holding a contested outcome). A single player's contest results stay a problem.
- Problems too: an unknown outcome, a contest (side B filled) outside competitive and cooperative-competitive games or with an empty side A, and results of mixed kinds (the milestones and every step use one kind; contest results go with a filled side B, and only then). player1 goes on side A of a contest they are in (side A is player1's side, or player1's camp with three players), the sides' results swapping with it; a contest player1 sits out keeps the sides the planner wrote, since which camp a seat is in lives in the setup's text and the planner is told player1's camp is side A (camps {player1, player3} against {player2} put player3 on side A). While the story has no thread plan (`Story.hasThreadAnalysis()`), a multiplayer plan must be one thread with every player, on a shared outcome when the story holds one (a contested shared one in a competitive game that holds one); the thread prompt's "MANDATORY FIRST THREAD REQUIREMENT" prints on the same condition.
- A story that holds no outcomes at all (one that started before the start check) skips every outcome rule; that is logged once per story per process. Repairs are logged as `[LLM] repair` lines (roles `switchAnalysis`, `threadAnalysis`); a plan that can't be used logs a warning without the problem, which names model-written ids.

**Contests have two sides (an accepted engine limit, owner, 2026-09-28).** A contest thread sets side A (`playersSideA`) against side B (`playersSideB`), and a contested outcome resolves as `sideAWins`, `mixed` or `sideBWins`; there is no contest of three or more parties, and none is planned ("Adding those would also touch outcomes, etc."). In a three-player competitive or cooperative-competitive story, a contest is between two camps (one of them two players), player1's camp on side A, each contest with one shared opposites scoreboard; there is no three-player race and no "who leads" string. Production's setup form follows it since 2026-09-28 (setup round 3, `prompts/setupPromptText.ts` and `setupSchema.ts`: two camps, one opposites scoreboard per contest, no race, no "who leads" string), and so do the planners (planner v2 with two-sided contests: a contest always has exactly two sides, with three players the setup's two camps, player1's camp on side A). At the ending, the scoreboard ending rule (`SCOREBOARD_ENDING_RULE` in `BeatPromptService.ts`, since 2026-09-28: the side ahead on a contested outcome's scoreboard wins unless its milestones clearly say otherwise, 45-55 a draw; since 2026-09-30 only once the contest is complete: an unfinished one has no winner, and the ending tells which side is ahead, neither between 45 and 55, and that it isn't settled) reads every contest this way, camps included; it prints on the ending of a story that plays contests (`contestsPlayable` in `core/utils/outcomeReadiness.ts`: two or more players, competitive or cooperative-competitive), holds a contested shared outcome and keeps a shared opposites stat (`takesScoreboardEndingRule`), and the setup's form says so in its three sentences about how outcomes are decided (contest setups only). The template editor offers the Contest kind only on a shared outcome of a World that plays contests (`worldPlaysContests`, by the World's game mode and highest player count), keeps it selectable on an outcome that already holds one, and its validation card warns about a contested shared outcome in the stories that can't play it (a cooperative or single-player World, or a competitive World's single-player stories).

**The option rules (B6, since 2026-09-28)** (`server/src/game/services/optionRules.ts`). A single player's challenge and contest chapter steps (`takesOptionRules`: the rolled turns) get three different ways to act a player can tell apart, the reward exception, a lever line the game computes from the player's history (`sacrificeRewardLine`: a sacrifice or reward fits when none was offered in the last two rolled turns, the other kind preferred) and no second sacrifice of a stat within a thread; in the fields, a base-point scale (0 sensible, +5 for an established lead, -5 to -15 for the tempting strength) and at most two stat bonuses from the option's own approach. Every other turn, and every group turn, is today's form (B6 was never built or measured for groups). Measured as the eval's `turnB6`; `adoptedTurns.test.ts` holds it byte for byte. **Difficulty under B6:** the balance simulation reads 7 points fewer favorable results if the scale were applied in full (45% → 37%), but the round's own B6 options kept today's odds (44%, the model gives a tempting option a small negative base and keeps its bonus), so the difficulty levels stay as they are. The paid final check (2026-09-28) read production's own options: 42.8% favorable against 44.7% on today's form, so they still stand; a strength option now has the best chance outright in 72% of challenge sets against 49%, a lean toward the stat option the owner's rating may weigh.

**The exploration-order line (since 2026-09-30, every player count)** (`EXPLORATION_ORDER` and `takesExplorationOrder` in `optionRules.ts`). On an exploration step the game records the chosen option by its position as the step's result (`BeatResolutionService`: option n is resolution n), and the next turn and the chapter's milestone follow that result. On a chapter step where some player's thread explores, the option instructions say so after the option types: a player's three options are the step's three results in order, each the same action in the same direction, and the beat text carries out none of them. Before, the turn was shown the three results with nothing saying its options were them, and wrote options that carried out another result or none, mostly on a chapter's first step (the playthroughs' New Avalon turns 6 and 14, food trucks turns 14 and 17). Measured as the eval's `choiceResult`. Groups (the choice-result stage): options at their own result 0 of 6 → 6 of 6, nothing else moved, waits level. A single player, adopted after a second run the same day (the choice-line-sp stage) with production's one retry of a short reply in the loop: on the replies the game keeps, options at their own result 12 of 20 → 19 of 20 (moved), nothing moved the wrong way, p95 waits including the retry 41.0 s for chapter steps and 34.4 s for openings (production beside it 59.3 s and 69.3 s, from its own retries). The first run's 3 of 20 one-paragraph replies with the line (production 0 of 20) came out the other way round there: the line 0 of 20, production 3 of 20, each rescued by the retry, so they read as Luna medium's base rate of short replies rather than the line's. `adoptedTurns.test.ts` holds the line to the measured form.

**A turn that comes back as one short paragraph** (`beatChecks.ts`, about 3% of Luna medium turns on every eval form) gets one more call told the problem (`checkedBeatReply`, in `AIStoryGenerator.generateBeats`); the paragraphs are those the client shows (`paragraphsOf`, a copy of `normalizeStoryText`). A second short reply is used: a short turn is better than a failed one; so is the first when the second call fails (after the chat model's own re-sends). The log line names the story and turn, or the error's class, never text. **A turn that comes back with no options** for a player (outside the ending, which shows none; `missingOptionsProblem`, since 2026-09-30, 1 of 64 production turns that morning) gets the same one call, told both problems when a reply has both (`beatReplyProblem`). A reply without options is never used, since the player could not go on: when the retry has none either, the turn fails (`UnusableResultError`), and so it does when the retry's call fails; the game then sends the failed turn once more (below). The one exception is a first reply that had options and was only short: it is used when the retry comes back without options or its call fails.

**The roll.** `BeatResolutionService.calculateTotalPoints` counts only the first `MAX_STAT_MODIFIERS_PER_OPTION` (2) stat bonuses, each clamped to ±`MAX_STAT_MODIFIER_POINTS` (15, `core/config.ts`); the player's point breakdown shows the clamped values, each bonus named by its stat's name (`Story.getStatById`), or by its id where no stat resolves (the beat repair above keeps that to ids it can't read). Stored options keep what the model wrote, so the eval's bonus-range check still measures the model.

**Thread steps.** Before each thread beat, `ThreadResolutionService.resolveCurrentThreads` writes every thread's result for the beat just played. `ThreadManager.updateThreadResolution` puts it at that thread's own first unresolved step, so parallel threads each record step k on beat k and get their result and milestone on their last beat.

- **Challenge and contest steps** combine every player's roll.
- **An exploration step** is a choice. Since 2026-09-30:
  - A thread on one player's own outcome goes the way that player chose, when they are in it.
  - Any other thread goes the way most of its players chose. A tie is broken by the game's dice (`Math.random`) among the tied choices, so a single player, the owner and a clear majority roll none.
  - Before, the first player listed decided for everyone. In a group thread on another player's outcome, player1's choice wrote that player's milestone, and the next turn narrated that player doing player1's choice (the playthroughs: 5 of 7 group exploration steps).

**Character selection.** `PlayerManager.setCharacterSelection` gives every player stat a value: a background stat the chosen background leaves out takes the stat's `initialValue` (read by type, below), or the template editor's default (`""`, `[]`, 50) when that is missing or doesn't fit. Stories already running are untouched.

**Background values by type** (`core/utils/statValueCheck.ts`). A background's starting value is checked against its stat's type: kept when it fits; converted when the reading is unambiguous (a numeric string for a number, percentage or opposites stat, percentages and opposites also as `"75%"`; `"a|b"` for opposites when a + b = 100, stored as a; a single string for a `string[]` stat, `""` as `[]`; a one-item list for a string stat); otherwise replaced by the stat's own `initialValue` read the same way, or the editor's default. A percentage or opposites value outside 0 to 100, number or string, is clamped to the range (120 becomes 100, `"-5"` becomes 0; logged as `clamped`); number stats have no range. A value whose stat id isn't a player stat (unknown, or a shared stat) is dropped. The check runs where values enter: `setCharacterSelection`, `createStoryStateFromTemplate` (story start), `StoryCreationService.generateStoryState` (a generated setup, before it is stored) and `TemplateService` create / update (a template, before it is written; ZIP imports are covered at story start). Each place logs one line with the story or template id and the counts per seat and background (`player1 background 0: 1 converted, 1 dropped`), no stat ids or values; once a place has fixed the values, the later places find nothing to log. The template editor shows the same reading as a `backgrounds` warning per background (`validateTemplateIntegrity`), saying what each value becomes; unknown stat ids stay with the existing reference error, shared stats in a background with the orphaned-stat warning.

**Story start** (`core/utils/outcomeReadiness.ts`). A story needs an outcome to play toward (shared, or on a seat in play), and a multiplayer story needs a shared outcome, because its first thread groups every player on one. `storyStartProblem` holds the rule; `templateStartProblem(template, playerCount)` reads a template or setup reply (only the seats in play count) and `storyStateStartProblem(state, playerCount)` a created state. `StoryCreationService.createStoryFromTemplate` refuses a template story that breaks it with a 400, before any DB entry. `generateStoryState` retries a generated setup that breaks it once (a fresh sample of the same request) and otherwise fails through the setup-failure path.

**A setup that fails for good** (`server/src/stories/setupStatus.ts`). `generateStoryState` records the failure (`setup_failed`), deletes the story's DB entries and logs one line with the story id only (the error can quote the premise). `GET /stories/:id/status` then answers `failed`; a story neither stored nor being set up in this process (a restart mid-setup) answers `failed` / `setup_lost`, so no client waits forever. The client stops polling, drops the story's codes from local storage and shows "We couldn't create this story. Please try again." (`StorySetupFailed`) with Try again (the same premise and settings, sent through moderation and the rate limit again) and Back (the setup form, still filled in). A content-filter refusal is answered before anything is queued and keeps its own moderation notification. Template stories are created before the response (`ready`, or an HTTP error on the form); the same screen covers them should a poll ever report a failure. Invited players whose story failed get the game page's invalid-code error. The template editor's validation card (`validateTemplateIntegrity`, category `outcomes`) shows the problem for each player count it stops: an error when no allowed count can start, a warning when some can, and a warning for an outcome id held in more than one list (milestones land on one copy only: the first `Story.getOutcomeById` finds, shared first). Stories already running are not checked here.

**A turn that fails** (the queue's failure path, `BaseQueueProcessor.resendFailed`; `TURN_RESENDS` in `GameQueueProcessor.ts`, since 2026-09-30). A `moveStoryForward` whose writing throws (`handleProgression`: a plan unusable twice, a beat reply unusable, a call that failed through the chat model's re-sends) is queued once more, at the back of its game's queue, and runs `handleProgression` again from the same story: its own safeguards run again too. Only the writing is resent: once storing the written turn has begun it may be stored, so a failure storing or sending it is reported (`TurnDeliveryError`) but never written again, and the turn's images and pregeneration, which start after it is stored and sent, never fail it (logged only). The operation counts its `sends`, so it never loops; a warning names the operation and the error's class. A second failure takes the failure path: `operationError`, which `GameHandler` sends to every player in the game as "Unable to continue the story. Please try again." (its listener had been lost in 2025-05, so no failure reached anyone; a failed pregeneration tells no one, since the turn is written when it is played). A player's own failed choice or character selection reaches that player as its friendly line ("Unable to process your choice…", "Unable to save your character choice…"), and its pending operation is rejected with that line too, since `makeChoice`/`selectCharacter` and `websocket.ts` send the rejection's message on; the error's own text is logged only. The queue sends nothing a third time; a player's "Try again" does (below). Before, a failed turn was never sent again (the playthroughs' group stories, stuck for good). No other operation is resent.

**Trying a failed turn again** (since 2026-09-30). The game screen shows the failure where it was waiting (`TurnFailedNotice`, the ui `Notification` in its error style with no close button, `role="alert"`): in place of the next beat's spinner, of "Setting up the story..." (the first turn, or the other players still picking), below the latest beat when the player's own choice failed and its options are open again, or above the character selection's confirm button, which stops showing "Processing…". The line is the server's; the button says "Try again". `turnFailureFrom` (`client/src/game/turnFailure.ts`) takes errors from the turn (`moveStoryForward`, `retry_turn`) and from the player's own choice or selection (queue or response), never an untyped or join-code error, and the notice stands until a state arrives in which the story moved on (a new beat, the player's choice or character stored; `stillFailed`), so when another player's retry works everyone's notice goes. "Try again" (`tryAgainRequest`, sent by `gameService.tryAgain`) sends what the story on screen is missing: the selection again while characters are picked, the player's choice again when it was never stored on that beat, else `retry_turn`. `GameHandler.retryTurn` queues `moveStoryForward` from the stored story only when it waits for its turn (every character picked, every choice in, `waitsForItsTurn`) and `GameQueueProcessor.isTurnOnItsWay` is false (no turn, choice or character selection queued or running, a resend included; `BaseQueueProcessor.hasUnfinished`), so any number of players pressing it queue one turn; otherwise it sends that player the story as stored. It counts against the `make_choice` rate limit; a limited retry brings the notice back. A player who arrives (a reload, a second device, a server restart mid-turn) at a story that waits for its turn with nothing on its way is told the same line (`tellIfTurnStuck`, after `verify_code`). Still open: the other players' notices stay up while one player's retry is being written, until its beat arrives.

**One retry** (`server/src/game/services/retryOnce.ts`). `withOneRetry(attempt, problemOf, what)` runs a model call, and when the result parses but has a problem, calls once more, passing the problem; a second problem throws `UnusableResultError` ("Failed to generate a usable <what>", the problem on `.problem`, never in the message). A call that fails outright is not retried there: the chat model already retries failed requests. The plan checks follow the same one-retry rule with their own flow, since a chapter length alone never fails the turn (above).
